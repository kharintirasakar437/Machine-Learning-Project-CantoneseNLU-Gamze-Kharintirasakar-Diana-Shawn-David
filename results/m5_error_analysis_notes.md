# Error Analysis

The sentiment error analysis shows that the neutral `ok` class is the main source of confusion. Of the 249 validation errors produced by the current logistic-regression model, 230 involve either a true or predicted `ok` label. Many of the sampled reviews contain mixed sentiment, such as strong praise combined with smaller criticisms about price, service, or individual dishes. These examples suggest that character n-gram features can identify local positive and negative expressions but may have difficulty determining the overall balance of sentiment across a longer review. Some errors are less ambiguous: several strongly negative reviews were predicted as positive, and some highly enthusiastic reviews were predicted as negative, indicating that not all mistakes can be attributed to borderline labels.

For language detection, the dominant error pattern is confusion with the code-mixed class. Of 583 validation errors, 234 Mandarin examples and 202 Cantonese examples were predicted as code-mixed, while direct Cantonese-to-Mandarin and Mandarin-to-Cantonese confusion occurred only once in each direction. This suggests that the model generally distinguishes the two pure-language classes well, but has greater difficulty determining when a sentence should be considered code-mixed. The sampled class-2 examples also show that mixing can be subtle and sometimes appears through only a few lexical or grammatical cues. Because the code-mixed data were synthetically generated and the dataset contains known label inconsistencies, some apparent errors may reflect ambiguous or noisy examples rather than purely model failure.

# Draft Conclusions

The current validation results show that classical machine-learning methods can provide useful baselines for both Cantonese sentiment analysis and language detection, although the two tasks present different challenges. For sentiment analysis, character-level TF-IDF with logistic regression remains the strongest known configuration at this stage of Milestone 5, with tuned Naive Bayes performing very similarly. Error analysis indicates that the main difficulty lies around the neutral `ok` class, which is involved in 230 of the model's 249 validation errors. Reviews containing both positive and negative comments appear especially difficult, although some strongly positive and strongly negative examples are also misclassified.

For language detection, tuning substantially improved Multinomial Naive Bayes compared with the earlier baseline. The dominant remaining error pattern is confusion between the pure Cantonese or Mandarin classes and the code-mixed class. Of 583 validation errors, 436 consist of Cantonese or Mandarin examples predicted as code-mixed, while direct Cantonese-to-Mandarin and Mandarin-to-Cantonese confusion occurs only once in each direction. This suggests that the model distinguishes the two pure language classes relatively well, while identifying the boundary between pure and code-mixed text remains substantially more difficult.

Overall, these results show that relatively simple character-based models can capture useful linguistic signals in Cantonese NLU tasks. However, the remaining errors also show the limitations of relying primarily on local character patterns when labels depend on the overall balance of sentiment or on subtle distinctions between pure and mixed-language text.

# Draft Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the current results. First, all Milestone 5 model selection and error analysis are based only on the validation split. The test split remains locked and will only be used for the final evaluation, so the current results should not be interpreted as final estimates of generalization performance.

Second, the sentiment dataset is based on restaurant reviews from OpenRice. This provides realistic Cantonese text, but limits the domain of the conclusions. Performance on restaurant reviews may not transfer directly to other forms of Cantonese sentiment, such as social media, news comments, or conversational text. Many reviews are also long and contain both positive and negative opinions about different aspects of a restaurant. A single document-level label may therefore hide substantial within-review variation.

Third, the language-detection task includes a synthetic code-mixed class. The error analysis shows that this class accounts for most of the remaining confusion. Some code-mixed examples contain only subtle changes from an otherwise pure Cantonese or Mandarin sentence, while other examples appear linguistically unusual. The project has also identified known label conflicts in the upstream language-detection data. As a result, some apparent model errors may reflect ambiguous, synthetic, or noisy examples rather than model failure alone.

Finally, the classical models evaluated in this project rely heavily on character-level statistical patterns. These features are computationally efficient and perform strongly as baselines, but they do not explicitly model longer-range meaning, discourse context, or the relationship between multiple opinions within a review. The frozen LaBSE plus kNN approach adds pretrained multilingual semantic representations, but its current validation performance does not outperform the strongest TF-IDF models.
